QA/Review Process Overhaul

I joined The Trevor Project's Training Team as one of two eLearning developers in June 2021 as two concurrent major curriculum refresh projects for the Lifeline and Digital Crisis Support teams were ongoing. Due to project delays resulting in a hard deadline for Lifeline, an external team managed the content QA, running directly behind the eLearning developers uploading SCORM modules for Lifeline's four-course training program to Litmos LMS. The QA was highly disorganized, assigning review criteria to 30+ individuals with their names and criteria only stated in a Google Sheet that wasn't communicated to reviewers, resulting in overlapping criteria and constantly changing reviewer assignments. This resulted in 1600+ rows of edits, many duplicated, that the eLearning developers implemented by making 800+ corrections each while simultaneously developing new Digital content. We were prohibited from sorting or filtering the sheet to identify duplicates resulting in a highly inefficient, four-week QA. Post-QA feedback and our Team's experience made it clear this was unsustainable and more efficient, streamlined QA strategy was needed.

QA/Review Process Flowchart

This four-phase workflow was designed to rectify the major issues our Training Team encountered including the lack of a clear workflow leading to continuous addition of edits, poor assignment design causing duplicate revisions, and failing to prioritize major content revisions prior to QA. The workflow sequences Internal Training Team tasks (blue) and External QA Team tasks (purple) per role in one process map. It delineates major and minor edits and enforces a cutoff of edit cycles by phase. Using Review 360 and shared CSVs in Phase I, stakeholders and leadership commented on major issues that Training revised prior to uploading to Litmos in Phase 2. This enabled a proper QA in Phase 3 focused on navigation errors, missing assets, and typos on the content in the LMS. The workflow also showed documentation export points (red outlined steps) to create receipt of all revisions being complete in an end-of-project "source of truth" Google Sheet.

The Training Team needed to implement a shared tracking tool where QA assignments were clearly described and assigned to QA tester volunteers by their managers, who Training would send notifications to during Phase 2. Communicating assignments and sending reminders resolved the issue of testers not knowing they had been assigned to QA or not having the bandwidth to complete their tasks. Our Team also needed to categorize and prioritize revisions, tackling time intensive edits and rewrites requiring IDs first. We also needed a simple tool that did not require training to use.

QA Tracker - Assignment & Validation System

The QA Tracker was created in a shared Google Sheet with the following sheets: testers assigned to QA assignments with steps to complete each assignment, module QA, assessment QA, and a validation sheet to create dropdown menus for testers to easily select their name and QA assignment criteria, such as the category of asset types they were QAing, the slide number of the error, a place to upload screenshots, and an issue box to describe errors and revisions. The validation sheet also enabled the Training Team to use permissions to split each module QA and assessment QA sheet into purple, designating the testers areas for filling in data, like in the process flowchart, and blue for the Training Team to assign revisions to the eLearning developer. The eLearning developer could stay on the same sheet as the testers' revisions, scrolling to the right to the Training Team data area, rapidly resolving errors and adding comments describing all revisions, prioritize revisions, and apply different revision statuses, sending automated email notifications to the ID manager for review and confirmation that edits were complete when a revision status was set to "Ready for Review". Four sheets were made for each course in the Digital Training Refresh QA and a read-only copy was saved for the end-of-project source documentation confirming the Training Team completed all revisions.

For team accountability and assessment of the new QA process implementation, the Training Team needed to compile all exported documentation from the end of Phase 1 and Phase 3 revision cycles to create one "source of truth" document for record that all major stakeholder revisions and minor QA edits were 100% resolved and to evaluate if the new QA process implementation improved on the previous QA process. Since all our export documentation downloaded in CSV format, a shared Google Sheet to compile our export documentation into one record was the most logical tool to use.
