QA/Review Process Overhaul

Upon joining The Trevor Project's Training team as one of two new eLearning developers, our first major assignment was delivering the Lifeline Crisis Team's four-course training program in Litmos LMS. The project was past deadline; thus, an all-hands QA team of volunteers from across the organization performed QA on content as eLearning developers uploaded it to the LMS. The QA was highly disorganized: 30+ reviewers and their QA assignments were listed by their leads in a single Google Sheet, many without being notified. Vague assignments and disabling sort/filter on the Google Sheet resulted in many duplicate/redundant edits and major content revisions, duplicates, and minor grammar fixes being mixed together. This resulted in 1,600+ rows of edits that the eDevs had to address row by row. Each eDev made 800+ corrections while simultaneously developing new content in a highly inefficient, four-week QA.

Problem: Lack of Workflow

Post-QA feedback and our Team's experience made it clear a more efficient, streamlined QA strategy was needed. To overhaul the current process, we needed a structured workflow between the Training and QA teams, with clear starts and stops to edit cycles to prevent scope creep, in which major content revisions were prioritized.

QA/Review Process Flowchart

This four-phase workflow sequences Internal Training Team tasks (blue) and External QA Team tasks (purple) per role in one process map. In Phase 1, stakeholders commented on major issues in Review360 and shared CSVs. Training resolved all major edits before uploading to Litmos in Phase 2. The QA in Phase 3 assigned reviewers to minor navigation errors, missing assets, and typos in the LMS content. Export points (red-outlined steps) marked where documentation was exported at each phase-end to aggregate into an end-of-project "source of truth" Google Sheet in the last phase.

Problem: Communicating Assignments & Tracking Edits

Training and QA teams needed a simple, shared tracking tool where Training created detailed, step-by-step QA assignments, team leads assigned these to reviewers, and Training could notify the assigned reviewers 1-2 weeks before the Phase 3 QA. For Training team members, we needed the ability to rapidly sort and prioritize edits so we could assign an ID or eDev to efficiently complete the revision.

QA Tracker - Assignment & Validation System

The QA Tracker was created in a shared Google Sheet with the following sheets: step-by-step QA assignments assigned to testers, module QA, assessment QA, and a validation sheet to create dropdown menus for testers to easily select their name and QA assignment criteria, such as the category of asset types they were QAing, the slide number of the error, a place to upload screenshots, and an issue box to describe errors and revisions. Like the process flowchart, each module QA and assessment QA sheet was split into purple, for testers' data inputs, and blue for Training's inputs, such as assigning revisions to a eLearning developer. Training's cells were restricted to use by our Team. Splitting one sheet meant the eLearning developer could address testers' revisions by scrolling right to the Training data inputs, rapidly resolve errors and add comments describing all revisions, prioritize edits, and apply different revision statuses, sending automated email notifications to the ID manager for review and confirmation that edits were complete when a revision status was set to "Ready for Review". Four sheets were made for each course in the Digital Training Refresh QA and a read-only copy was saved for the end-of-project source documentation confirming the Training Team completed all revisions.

Problem: Project Completion & Accountability

For team accountability and assessment of the new QA process implementation, the Training Team needed to compile all exported documentation from the end of Phase 1 and Phase 3 revision cycles to create one "source of truth" document for record that all major stakeholder revisions and minor QA edits were 100% resolved and to evaluate if the new QA process implementation improved on the previous QA process. Since all our export documentation downloaded in CSV format, a shared Google Sheet to compile our export documentation into one record was the most logical tool to use.

Source Documentation - Complete Revision Tracking

The Training Team exported Phase 1 documentation of Review 360 comments for all four Digital Training Refresh courses. Review 360 comments contained stakeholder edits, each followed by a Training Team comment describing the resolution they applied, and confirmed by the ID manager in their final review by resolving the comment. The assessments were already in Google Sheets; thus, we imported the data into our "source of truth" document. For future troubleshooting, we created Sheets per each module and assessment with only Training Team comments and resolutions isolated from the Review 360 exports and assessment CSVs as well. Phase 3 QA export documentation, including the QA Assignments and QA Tracker, was already in spreadsheet format; thus we imported the Phase 3 sheet data into our "source of truth" document. The Crisis Support Team manager who managed and assigned the QA tester provided us with a CSV export of their side of the project in Asana to create a complete cross-functional record of all team members, teams, and tasks involved in the project.
